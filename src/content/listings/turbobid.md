## What it is

TurboBid is Windows desktop estimating software for electrical and plumbing contractors, sold as separate electrical and plumbing editions. You build estimates from a database of items and assemblies with labor units, account for labor burden, overhead, and direct job costs (permits, tools, subcontracts, bonds), and produce a bid package and purchase orders. It handles standard plan-based estimates and service work, including flat-rate pricing.

TurboCloud is TurboBid's online database service. It syncs items and assemblies and pulls updated material prices, and it connects to supply-house price sources such as TurboPricer, NetPricer, Trade Service Tra-Ser, EPIC, and vendor price update files.

TurboBid's own takeoff screen is for entering quantities, broken down by room or location. To measure from PDFs on screen, it integrates with [PlanSwift](../planswift/), which sends counts and lengths straight into TurboBid. Without PlanSwift, TurboBid says you can mark up prints in a PDF viewer and type the counts in yourself.

## Who it's for

TurboBid says it's built for "the residential and light commercial sectors," covering new construction, remodeling, service work, and flat-rate pricing. It fits a solo electrician or plumber, or a small shop, that wants trade-specific labor units instead of building every assembly in a spreadsheet. On r/electricians, contractors describe it as a simple, lower-cost starting point for housing and small commercial work, and say they moved to heavier tools like ConEst IntelliBid as they grew.

If you mostly need quick, good-looking quotes from your phone and don't bid from plans, an estimate-and-invoice app like [Joist](../joist/) is cheaper and simpler.

## Pricing (verified October 30, 2026)

Electrical and plumbing editions are priced the same.

| Option | Price | What the vendor says is included |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly subscription | $135/mo, plus a mandatory $350 one-time setup fee | 1 free month of TurboCloud. The setup fee is refunded if you later convert to a purchase. |
| 4 monthly payments | $399/mo for 4 months | 6 free months of updates and support, 1 free month of TurboCloud |
| One-time purchase | $1,395 (listed regular price $1,620) | 6 free months of updates and support, 1 free month of TurboCloud |

Add-ons sold on the same site:

- **TurboCloud** (database sync and material price updates): $99/mo after the free first month, $535 for 6 months, or $949 for a year (listed regular price $1,070). It auto-renews and isn't refundable.
- **Software upgrades and support**: $350 for 1 year or $200 for 6 months. If your plan lapsed and new versions came out in the meantime, TurboBid adds a one-time $200 upgrade fee.
- **PlanSwift on-screen takeoff**, ordered through TurboBid: $1,750 (listed regular price $2,250).

TurboBid offers a 14-day free trial and says you can extend it another 14 days. It doesn't offer refunds, "since we offer a free 14-day trial."

**Out-of-date prices elsewhere:** Some third-party roundups still list TurboBid at $99/mo. The vendor's store showed $135/mo when we checked.

## Platforms

TurboBid runs on Windows. On a Mac, TurboBid says you first need Parallels ($99.99) and Microsoft Windows 11 ($139.00). TurboCloud handles syncing items, assemblies, and prices, but the estimating program itself is desktop software.

## What we couldn't verify

- Whether the $135/mo subscription includes ongoing upgrades and support. The purchase and 4-payment options list 6 free months, but the subscription page doesn't say.
- What the separate "Electrical Database" and "Plumbing Database" products ($1,395 each) are for. The store pages don't explain them.
- TurboBid's claims that its labor units and pricing are "accurate." We haven't tested estimates against real jobs.
